Impulse Noise

Impulse, or more descriptively called Salt And Pepper Noise, is a type of noise often seen in digital image degradation, where pixel data is noticeably corrupted. It's often seen as a collection of sparsely spread off-colored pixels on an otherwise normal image.

Impulse Noise is also called Salt And Pepper Noise becuase, on a grayscale image, the off-colored pixels give off the appear of particles of salt (white pixels) and pepper (black pixels) spread across the image.

There are two main methods of intentionally recreating this effect: Uniform and Impulse.

Uniform Impulse Noise

Uniform Impulse Noise follows uniform probability. Unlike typical noise algorithms, which use a probability to randomly generate pixel values, Salt And Pepper Noise uses probability to randomly decide if a pixel should be affected or not.

This method works by generating a random floating point number (between [0,1]). This number is then compaired with a given 'density' parameter. If the random number is less than the density, draw the pixel ontop of the image. Otherwise, leave the pixel unaffected. The higher the 'density', the more pixels are expected to be affected.

How the pixels are effects is dependant on the algorithm. You could combine this with another noise method, to provide randomness in the effected pixels. Alternatively, you could also refer to the orginal image, blurring at the selected pixel locations, or mixing all neighbor pixel's colors, instead.

For the purpose of this website, and as it is the standard approach to Salt And Pepper Noise, all affected pixels are replaced by a soild color.

Clustered Impulse Noise

Clustered Impulse Noise is near identical to it's uniform variant. However, instead of running the probablity checks on every pixel of an image, the algorithm first randomly select 'k' pixel positions within the image. Then, within a range of 'radius' pixels around each selected position, we perform the Uniform Impulse Noise algorithm.

The parameters 'k', 'radius', and 'density' clearly affect the graph. However, you can also change how probabilty decides which pixel to effect. For example, unlike traditional Uniform Impulse Noise, you could add a bias to make pixels closer to the center of clusters more likely to be affected.
